### Q1 — Is AI Pre-Scan intended for market placement, or is it (for now) a tool the builder alone runs internally?

**Why it matters:** The EU AI Act's obligations attach to a *provider placing a system on the market or putting it into service* (Art. 3(3)–(4)) and to *deployers*. If the tool never leaves the builder's own hands, most obligations are prospective, not live. The proposal and README are written entirely in the present/near-future tense around a named persona ("Maria," a six-person compliance consultancy) and GTM sprints for adviser pilots — which reads as a genuine market-placement intent, not a thought experiment.

**Provisional assumption:** Treating this as a system being actively built *for* market placement to compliance advisers (i.e., "provider" obligations are the relevant frame, even pre-launch), since the documentation consistently designs for that buyer rather than for solo internal use.

### Q2 — Does the "quoted passage" evidence in a report ever concern an identifiable natural person in a way that functions as an assessment *of that person* (e.g., a named HR/recruitment lead, a named individual tied to a system decision) — or is every finding strictly about the organisation?

**Why it matters:** The brief is explicit that "the subject of research is the organisation, not any individual," and `LESSONS.md` acknowledges quotes "may name someone" incidentally. If a finding could ever be read as characterizing an individual's conduct or role rather than the company's system, that shifts both the GDPR analysis (special-category or profiling risk) and edges toward Annex III employment-domain territory rather than staying safely at "company research."

**Provisional assumption:** Findings are, by design and by the schema (`docs/report-spec.md` rule 1: "no regime vocabulary," subject = organisation), scoped to companies, and any named individuals appear only incidentally inside quoted source text, not as the subject of a finding.

### Q3 — What editorial step, if any, does the adviser (deployer) apply to the AI-generated report before it is handed to a client — is it reviewed and edited with the adviser taking responsibility for the content, or forwarded largely as generated?

**Why it matters:** This determines whether the Article 50(4) exemption from the synthetic-content marking duty (human review + editorial responsibility) applies to the report the adviser hands over, or whether the report — generated by GPT-4o and distributed to a third party (the client) — needs an explicit "this content was AI-generated" disclosure under Article 50(2). The README says "she hands them to clients"; it doesn't describe her editing the findings, only reading and acting on them.

**Provisional assumption:** The adviser reads and relies on the report but does not materially rewrite its findings (the standing legal notice is explicitly stated as "not editable by any generation step" — `docs/report-spec.md` rule 7) — so I am provisionally treating this as *not* meeting the Art. 50(4) editorial-responsibility bar, pending confirmation.
